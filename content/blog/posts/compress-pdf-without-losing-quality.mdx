Large PDFs are a pain. Emails bounce, uploads time out, and storage fills up fast. The good news is that most PDFs shrink dramatically with the right technique - files packed with high-resolution images can often drop by half or more while text stays perfectly readable.

## Why PDFs get so big

Before you compress, it helps to know where the size comes from:

1. **Images** - photos and scans stored at high resolution
2. **Embedded fonts** - every font used in the document is included
3. **Metadata** - hidden data like editor names and timestamps
4. **Repeated content** - duplicate pages or objects saved multiple times

For most files, images are the biggest culprit.

## The safe way to compress

The safest approach is to let a compressor apply sensible defaults: re-compress images at a high-quality setting, strip unnecessary metadata, and remove duplicate fonts - all without touching the text layer.

<InfoBox type="tip" title="Choose the right compression level">
If a PDF is going to be printed, keep compression light. If it is being emailed or posted online, stronger compression is almost always fine because screens hide the difference.
</InfoBox>

The exact result depends on the file, but as a rough example, a 12 MB scanned document might come down to something like this:

| Compression level | Approximate size | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light | 9 MB | Printing and archiving |
| Medium | 5 MB | Email attachments |
| Strong | 2.5 MB | Websites and messengers |

## What to check after compressing

After compression, open the file and verify three things:

- **Text is still selectable** - you can highlight and copy words
- **Images look acceptable** at the size you plan to share
- **Pages are all present** - nothing was dropped or merged

## Common compression mistakes

- **Over-compressing for print** - heavy compression can make photos look soft on paper
- **Expecting more from a text-only PDF** - if the file has few images, there is little to remove
- **Forgetting the original** - keep the source file until you are happy with the result
- **Uploading private files carelessly** - only use compressors that process files locally
