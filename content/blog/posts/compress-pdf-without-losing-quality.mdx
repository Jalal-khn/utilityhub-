Large PDFs are a pain. Emails bounce, uploads time out, and storage fills up fast. The good news is that most PDFs shrink dramatically with the right technique - often by 50 to 90 percent.

## Why PDFs get so big

Before you compress, it helps to know where the size comes from:

1. **Images** - photos and scans stored at high resolution
2. **Embedded fonts** - every font used in the document is included
3. **Metadata** - hidden data like editor names and timestamps
4. **Repeated content** - duplicate pages or objects saved multiple times

For most files, images are the biggest culprit.

## The safe way to compress

The safest approach is to let a compressor apply smart defaults: re-compress images at a high-quality setting, strip unnecessary metadata, and remove duplicate fonts.

<InfoBox type="tip" title="Choose the right compression level">
If a PDF is going to be printed, keep compression light. If it is being emailed or posted online, stronger compression is almost always fine because screens hide the difference.
</InfoBox>

Here is a typical result table from compressing a 12 MB scanned document:

| Compression level | Final size | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low | 9 MB | Printing and archiving |
| Medium | 5 MB | Email attachments |
| High | 2.5 MB | Websites and messengers |

## What to check after compressing

After compression, open the file and verify three things:

- **Text is still selectable** - you can highlight and copy words
- **Images look acceptable** at the size you plan to share
- **Pages are all present** - nothing was dropped or merged

<InfoBox type="success" title="Compression is usually invisible">
For screen use, a 60-80% size reduction is usually indistinguishable from the original. Most people only notice a difference when zooming deep into photos.
</InfoBox>

## Frequently asked questions

### Is compressing a PDF the same as converting it?

No. Compression reduces file size while keeping the PDF format. Conversion changes the file into another format, like Word or an image, which can actually increase the size.

### Will my scanned PDF compress well?

Almost always. Scans contain large uncompressed images, so even modest compression saves significant space.

### Can I compress a PDF on my phone?

Yes. A browser-based compressor works on any device, so you can shrink a file and send it without leaving your phone's browser.
