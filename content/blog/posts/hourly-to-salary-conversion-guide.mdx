Job offers, budgeting apps, loan applications - they all want an annual number. But if you are paid by the hour, translating that rate into a yearly figure involves more choices than most people realize: paid time off, overtime, and unpaid weeks all change the answer.

This guide covers the **standard hourly-to-salary formulas**, worked examples at common wages, and the adjustments that make your estimate match reality.

## The standard formula

**Annual salary = hourly rate x hours per week x weeks per year**

The conventional full-time baseline assumes:

- **40 hours per week**
- **52 weeks per year**
- = **2,080 hours per year**

So the quick mental shortcut is simply: *hourly rate x 2,080*.

## Adjustment 1: unpaid time off

The 52-week multiplier assumes payment for every week of the year. Hourly workers without paid vacation should adjust downward:

| Scenario | Weeks paid | $25/hour annual |
|---|---|---|
| Paid vacation (salaried-style) | 52 | $52,000 |
| 2 weeks unpaid | 50 | $50,000 |
| 4 weeks unpaid | 48 | $48,000 |

If you get paid vacation, keep 52 weeks - your employer pays for those weeks too. Freelancers and seasonal workers should use their realistic working weeks, not 52.

## Adjustment 2: overtime

US non-exempt employees earn 1.5x their base rate beyond 40 hours weekly. Overtime adds up fast because it compounds on every hour:

**Example:** $22/hour with consistent 6 overtime hours weekly:

- Base: $22 x 40 x 52 = $45,760
- Overtime: $33 x 6 x 52 = $10,296
- **Realistic annual total: ~$56,056**

That overtime represents over 22% of actual income - leaving it out badly understates both earnings and the cost of losing that job.

## Adjustment 3: part-time schedules

## Comparing a job offer: hourly vs salary

You are offered $60,000 salaried or $28/hour ($58,240 at 2,080 hours). Which wins? Check four things:

1. **Expected real hours.** Salaried roles averaging 45+ hours effectively pay less per hour than they appear.
2. **Benefits value.** Health insurance worth $6,000/year changes everything.
3. **Paid time off.** Hourly workers sometimes go unpaid for holidays; salaries do not.
4. **Overtime eligibility.** Salaried does not automatically mean exempt from overtime law - but many salaried roles are.

A $58,240 hourly offer with overtime potential and identical benefits can beat a $60,000 salary with a 50-hour norm.

## From annual back to hourly

Reverse the math when comparing a salaried offer to your current wage:

**Hourly equivalent = salary / 2,080**

- $52,000 / 2,080 = $25.00
- $65,000 / 2,080 = $31.25 - so a $31.25 hourly rate earns exactly $65,000 a year
- $78,000 / 2,080 = $37.50

Then divide further if useful: $52,000 ≈ $4,333 monthly ≈ $1,000 weekly ≈ $200 daily.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much is $50,000 a year per hour?**
$50,000 / 2,080 = about $24.04 per hour. Monthly it is roughly $4,167 gross before deductions.

**Do I multiply by 52 or 50 weeks?**
Use 52 if your vacation and holidays are paid; use fewer weeks only when time off is genuinely unpaid. Most employed people should use 52.

**Is $25 an hour good pay?**
Against the US median hourly wage, $25 sits above the middle - roughly $52,000 annually. Whether it feels comfortable depends heavily on local living costs and household size.

**How much is minimum wage annually?**
At the US federal minimum of $7.25, full-time work yields about $15,080 per year. Many states set higher floors - check your state's current rate.

**What about taxes on hourly income?**
Hourly wages are taxed identically to salary. Federal brackets, state tax, Social Security, and Medicare all apply the same way regardless of pay structure.

**Why does my paycheck differ from the calculated amount?**
Gross-to-net differences come from federal and state withholding, FICA taxes, benefit premiums, and retirement contributions. Your W-4 elections drive much of the variation.
