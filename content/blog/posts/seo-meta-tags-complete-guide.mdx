Meta tags are the small pieces of HTML that tell search engines what your page is about. Getting them right is one of the fastest SEO wins available, because they control how your pages appear in search results and on social media.

<InfoBox type="tip" title="Start with the basics">
Before worrying about schema markup or Twitter cards, make sure every page has a well-written title tag and meta description. These two tags are the foundation of on-page SEO.
</InfoBox>

## What are meta tags?

Meta tags live in the `<head>` section of your HTML document. They don't appear on the page itself, but search engines and social platforms read them to understand and display your content.

The four tags you should care about most are:

1. **Title tag** - the clickable headline in search results
2. **Meta description** - the summary text under the headline
3. **Open Graph tags** - how links look when shared on Facebook, WhatsApp, and X
4. **Twitter Card tags** - how links look when shared on X

## How to write a great meta title

Your title tag is the biggest factor in whether someone clicks your result. Good titles are:

- **50-60 characters** long
- **Specific** about what the page offers
- **Unique** on every page of your site
- Front-loaded with the primary keyword

Here is what a strong title tag looks like:

```html
<title>How to Compress a PDF (Free Online) | UtilityHub</title>
```

## How to write a meta description

A meta description is your chance to sell the click. Write 150-160 characters that summarize the page and include a call to action.

<InfoBox type="warning" title="No duplicate descriptions">
Search engines ignore duplicate meta descriptions. If two pages share the same description, Google will often generate its own snippet instead.
</InfoBox>

## Open Graph and Twitter Cards

When someone shares your link, Open Graph tags control the title, description, and image that appear. Without them, platforms guess what to show - and they often guess wrong.

| Tag | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `og:title` | Title shown in the share card |
| `og:description` | Description shown in the share card |
| `og:image` | Preview image (1200x630 recommended) |
| `og:url` | Canonical URL of the shared page |
| `twitter:card` | Card type, usually `summary_large_image` |

## Frequently asked questions about meta tags

### Do meta keywords still work?

The meta keywords tag was deprecated years ago and is ignored by Google, Bing, and most other major engines. Skip it and spend your effort on the title, description, and content quality instead.

### Should every page have unique meta tags?

Yes. Unique titles and descriptions for every page prevent internal cannibalization and give each URL its best chance to rank for its own query.
