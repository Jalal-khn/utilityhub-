A 60-page report when you only need the summary, a scanned contract bundled with someone else's paperwork, a form where the signature pages must be returned separately - large PDFs are rarely wanted whole. **Splitting a PDF** extracts the pages you actually need into a new, smaller file while leaving the original untouched.

This guide shows how to split a PDF by any page range, how to pull out single pages, and how to avoid the mistakes that make people re-do the job.

## Why split a PDF instead of sharing it whole?

Splitting solves everyday document problems that forwarding the full file cannot:

- **Privacy** - a contract's terms pages are shareable, but the appendix containing employee salaries is not
- **Relevance** - colleagues need chapter 4 of the manual, not all twelve chapters
- **File size** - email attachment limits are easier to meet when you send only required pages
- **Separate workflows** - signature pages go back to legal while reference pages stay with you
- **Clean records** - extracting your own statement pages from a combined bank download

In each case, the recipient gets a focused document, and you avoid exposing content they should not see.

## What happens when you split

A split reads your chosen page numbers from the source PDF and writes them into brand-new files. Three properties matter:

1. **The original is never modified.** Splitting produces new documents; the source stays complete.
2. **Quality is identical.** Pages are copied, not re-rendered - selectable text remains selectable, images keep their resolution.
3. **Ranges define the output.** You control exactly which pages land in which file, whether that is one continuous block or several separate extractions.

## How to split a PDF step by step

A browser-based splitter handles this without installing anything:

1. Open the **Split PDF** tool.
2. Select the PDF you want to divide.
3. Enter the page range or ranges you want to extract - for example, `3-7` for a block of pages, or `5-5` for a single page.
4. Start the split and download the new file.
5. Open the result and confirm it contains exactly the pages you expected.

## Choosing the right way to divide a document

Different goals call for different splits:

| Goal | Range to use | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Send only the executive summary | `1-4` | One small file with the opening section |
| Return signed pages from a form | `2-3` | Just the signature pages |
| Share a single invoice from a bundle | the invoice's page number, e.g. `8-8` | A clean one-page PDF |
| Divide a long manual into chapters | one split per chapter range | Separate files per topic |
| Remove a stray blank or misprinted page | split around it, then merge the good parts | A cleaned-up document |

That last row is worth remembering: **splitting plus merging** is the standard repair kit for PDFs. Extract the sections you want to keep, discard the rest, and combine them back together in order - see our guide on [how to merge PDF files](/blog/how-to-merge-pdf-files).

## Page numbering tips before you split

Most split confusion comes from counting pages wrong:

- **Page numbers in the tool refer to physical positions** (1st page, 2nd page...), not the printed folio numbers. If a document's numbered content starts after a cover and a table of contents, "page 1" of the text may be physical page 3.
- **Preview before you commit.** Scroll the source PDF, note exactly which physical pages contain what you need, and write the numbers down.
- **Watch for hidden blank pages.** Scans frequently include empty trailing pages that shift every subsequent number.

Taking thirty seconds to verify page positions saves a second round of splitting when the first output misses a page.

## Common splitting mistakes

- **Off-by-one ranges** caused by ignoring cover pages - always confirm against the actual page preview
- **Expecting the original to change** - splitting never edits the source; if you want a trimmed version to *replace* the original, delete the old file yourself after checking the new one
- **Splitting sideways scans** without fixing orientation first - straighten pages with UtilityHub's Rotate PDF tool so the extracted pages arrive right side up
- **Sending sensitive ranges carelessly** - remember that extracted files carry the same confidentiality as the source
- **Forgetting bookmarks and links may not survive** page boundaries - internal links pointing outside an extracted range stop working, which usually does not matter for short excerpts but can matter for long manuals

## Privacy considerations

Extracted pages are often the most sensitive part of a document - the salary schedule, the medical results, the signed agreement. Browser-based splitting keeps those pages local: your file is read directly from disk, processed in memory, and written back as a new file. Nothing uploads anywhere, so nothing lingers on a third-party server.
