WebP is a modern image format that produces dramatically smaller files than JPEG or PNG at the same quality. For most websites, switching your photos and graphics to WebP is one of the fastest wins for page speed — often cutting image weight by a third or more.

This guide explains how WebP compares to older formats, and how to convert images to WebP directly in your browser without uploading a file anywhere.

## Why WebP matters for website performance

Images usually make up the bulk of a page's weight. A page of uncompressed PNGs can take many megabytes to load, which directly hurts:

- **Load time** — smaller files download faster, especially on mobile networks.
- **Core Web Vitals** — the Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) score, a Google ranking factor, is heavily influenced by how fast the hero image loads.
- **Data usage** — visitors in areas with limited or expensive data benefit from smaller pages.

WebP attacks this problem with better compression than the formats that came before it. The result is the same picture at a fraction of the bytes.

## WebP vs JPEG vs PNG

| Format | Best for | Compression | Transparency | Typical savings vs JPEG |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **JPEG** | Photographic images | Lossy | No | Baseline |
| **PNG** | Screenshots, graphics, text | Lossless | Yes | — |
| **WebP (lossy)** | Photos on the web | Lossy (very efficient) | No | 25–35% smaller |
| **WebP (lossless)** | Graphics with transparency | Lossless (efficient) | Yes | 26%+ smaller than PNG |

The practical rule: **photos become lossy WebP, and graphics with flat colors or transparency become lossless WebP.** Both are smaller than their classic counterparts.

## When to keep the original format

WebP is right for the vast majority of web images in 2026, but a few cases still justify other formats:

- **Very old browsers** — browsers from before 2020 may not render WebP. If your audience includes those, serve JPEG/PNG as a `<picture>` fallback.
- **Editing workflows** — keep an original editable master (like a RAW file or layered PNG) and export WebP for the web.
- **Client deliverables** — if you must hand over a JPG or PNG file, convert WebP back using a dedicated converter.

The cleanest setup: keep the master file, convert a WebP copy for the site, and convert it back to PNG or JPG whenever a specific deliverable requires it.

## FAQ

### Does WebP work on all devices in 2026?
Yes, in practice. Every modern browser on Windows, macOS, Android, and iOS supports WebP. Only a small share of very old browsers require a fallback, which an `<picture>` element handles automatically.

### Will converting to WebP break transparency?
No, as long as you use lossless WebP. Transparent PNGs convert cleanly to lossless WebP with transparency intact and a smaller file.

### How much smaller are WebP files really?
Lossy WebP is typically 25–35% smaller than JPEG at equivalent visual quality, and lossless WebP is often 26% or more smaller than PNG. On image-heavy pages this translates directly to faster loading.
