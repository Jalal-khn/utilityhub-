Every social platform has its own limits on how much text a post can hold — but the number that matters most is almost never the hard limit. It is how much text the platform shows in the feed before cutting the rest behind a "more" link.

This guide walks through the character and word limits for the major platforms, explains why short posts usually perform better, and shows how to measure your drafts with a word counter before you hit publish.

## Why text length affects reach

Social feeds are built to show a preview, not the whole post. When a post is longer than what the interface displays, platforms collapse it behind a link — and most people never click it. That means your hook, your point, or your call to action can disappear below the fold.

Length also affects how often a post gets read or watched to completion, which many algorithms treat as a positive signal. Short, scannable posts are read to the end more often; long walls of text are abandoned. The rule of thumb: **show the point immediately, and keep extra detail below the fold anyway**.

## Character limits at a glance

| Platform | Hard limit | Preview before truncation | Practical sweet spot |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| X (Twitter) | 280 | 280 | 130–220 |
| Instagram caption | 2,200 | ~125 | ~100–220 |
| Threads | 500 | ~140 | ~80–250 |
| LinkedIn post | 3,000 | ~200–250 | ~150–300 |
| Facebook post | ~63,000 | ~480 | ~80–120 |
| TikTok description | 2,200 | ~80 | ~50–120 |
| YouTube description | 5,000 | ~100 (first line) | first line + links |

<InfoBox type="warning" title="Numbers change">
Limits are adjusted periodically and can vary by app version and interface. Always treat a list like this as a guide — verify against the exact box you are typing into, and never build a post that depends on landing right at a limit.
</InfoBox>

## The "preview-first" principle

The most reliable length strategy on every platform is the same:

1. **Put the hook in the first line** — one sentence that states the value so the reader instantly knows what the post is about.
2. **Put the call to action next** — the ask belongs in the visible area, not the cut-off section.
3. **Use whitespace** — short lines and line breaks make long-ish posts readable even when partly truncated.
4. **Link at the edge, not the center** — when you include a URL, it counts toward character limits, so place it where losing a few characters hurts least.

## Platform-specific notes

- **X (Twitter):** URLs and @mentions count toward the 280 limit; media does not. Keep the message short and leave room to reply to comments.
- **Instagram:** Hashtags count as part of the caption but do not help reach as much as they once did. Put the value in the first two lines, then a spacer, then hashtags.
- **LinkedIn:** The first ~200–250 characters set the "See more" preview — that is where your strongest sentence belongs. Longer posts that open with a strong line still perform well.
- **Facebook:** Feed truncation around ~480 characters means most readers only ever see a short part. Very short posts with a clear ask typically out-perform long ones.
- **YouTube/TikTok:** The first line is the metadata that appears in search and around the video. Write the first line as a standalone summary, then expand.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do hashtags count toward the character limit?
Yes, on every platform that counts characters, hashtags are part of the caption text. Keep count for any hashtag-based limit, and avoid pasting dozens of tags when a related handful does the job.

### Is there an ideal word count for social media posts?
There is no universal ideal, but the feed preview should contain your full point. A practical range for most posts is 50–175 words; above that, structure with headings or line breaks and keep the ask near the top.

### Do links count toward character limits?
Yes, on X and Threads links (including shortened ones) consume characters. On Instagram and Facebook links in captions may not be clickable at all — put them in the bio or first comment instead.

## Conclusion

Text length on social media is not about obeying the hard limit — it is about fitting the point into what people actually see in the feed. A short hook, an early call to action, and whitespace will outperform a longer post with the same message almost every time.
