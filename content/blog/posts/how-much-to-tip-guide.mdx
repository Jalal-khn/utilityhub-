Tipping causes genuine anxiety: too little feels embarrassing, too much strains the budget, and the rules seem to change at every counter, cab, and coffee shop. This guide removes the guesswork with clear **tipping percentages for every common situation**, the math behind them, and how customs differ worldwide.

## The quick reference table

| Service | Standard tip | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Table service (US) | 15-20% | 20%+ for excellent service |
| Bartender | $1-2 per drink or 15-20% | Tab or per-drink both fine |
| Food delivery | 15-20% or $3-5 min | More in bad weather |
| Barista / counter | $0-1 or spare change | Optional, always appreciated |
| Barber / stylist | 15-20% | Skip if owner? Modern view: no need |
| Taxi / rideshare | 10-15% | Round up for short trips |
| Hotel housekeeping | $2-5 per night | Daily, since staff rotate |
| Buffet service | 10% | They still bring drinks, clear plates |

## Restaurant tipping: the detailed math

The core formula is simple:

**Tip = bill x tip percentage**

On an $86.50 dinner:

- 15% = $12.98 (adequate service)
- 18% = $15.57 (good service)
- 20% = $17.30 (excellent service)
- Total with 20% tip: $103.80

### Pre-tax or post-tax?

Sales tax runs 6-10% depending on location. Tipping on the pre-tax subtotal at 20% equals tipping on the post-tax total at about 18.3% - a difference of roughly $1.50 on this bill. Etiquette prefers pre-tax; convenience prefers the final number. Either choice lands within normal range.

### What the percentage really buys

In much of the US, servers earn a tipped minimum wage as low as $2.13-$5 per hour before tips. Your tip is not a bonus on top of fair pay - it *is* most of their pay. That context explains why American norms run far higher than elsewhere and why skipping the tip punishes the worker, not the restaurant.

## Splitting the bill without friction

Group dinners create math anxiety worse than the tipping itself. Two clean approaches:

**Method 1 - equal split:** Add tip to the whole bill, divide by heads.
$430 bill + 18% tip ($77.40) = $507.40 / 5 people = **$101.48 each**

**Method 2 - itemized:** Everyone pays for what they ordered plus their share of tax and tip. Fairer but slower - agree on the method *before* ordering, not when the check arrives.

## Tipping around the world

| Region | Norm |
|---|---|
| United States / Canada | 15-20% expected at restaurants |
| Western Europe | Service usually included; round up or 5-10% |
| UK | 10-12.5% if no service charge added |
| Japan | No tipping - can cause confusion or offense |
| South Korea | Not customary; increasingly accepted in tourist zones |
| Australia / New Zealand | Not expected; rounded-up bills for great service |
| Middle East | Varies; 10-15% in upscale tourist venues |

Tipping 20% in Tokyo does not signal generosity - it signals that you do not know local custom. Five minutes of research before travel prevents awkward moments.

## Beyond restaurants

- **Salon color/chemical services:** tip each professional separately if multiple people work on you ($5-10 to the assistant who washes).
- **Movers:** $20-40 per person for a half-day move, more for stairs or heavy items.
- **Furniture/appliance delivery:** $5-20 depending on difficulty.
- **Valet:** $2-5 at pickup.
- **Holiday season:** consider a week's pay for regular caregivers, cleaners, or dog walkers.

## When service is genuinely bad

Reduce the tip, but understand what it touches. Problems beyond a server's control - slow kitchen, wrong menu prices, understaffing - are not their fault, and punishing them compounds it. For truly poor service directly caused by your server, 10% with a polite note to the manager communicates more than zero. Only outright rudeness justifies leaving nothing in cultures where tipping is the wage.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is 15% still an acceptable tip?**
Yes for ordinary service, though urban norms have drifted toward 18-20%. On small bills, rounding up generously costs little and offsets the servers handling low-check tables.

**Should I tip on alcohol separately?**
Standard practice tips on the full bill including drinks. Some etiquette guides suggest 10-15% on expensive bottles since effort differs little from a $40 wine - use judgment.

**Do I tip the owner of a salon or restaurant?**
Traditional etiquette said no; modern practice says yes if they performed the service. Owners increasingly rely on tips like everyone else.

**How does tipping work with coupons or discounts?**
Tip on the pre-discount value of what you received. A half-price meal still required the same work - tip on what the meal would normally cost.

**What about self-service kiosks asking for tips?**
Tipping for counter pickup where you bus your own table is genuinely optional. Zero is a legitimate choice; small change remains appreciated but never obligatory.

**Is it rude to tip in cash?**
Not at all - cash is immediate, certain, and sometimes preferred by workers. Adding it on the card is fine too; either reaches the staff.
