Images come in many formats, and the wrong one causes real problems: photos that will not upload, logos with ugly white boxes, iPhone pictures that websites refuse to open. Converting an image to the right format solves all of these - and it takes seconds in your browser.

This guide covers **how to convert images online** between the four formats that matter most - **JPG, PNG, WEBP, and HEIC** - and which one you should choose for photos, logos, screenshots, and the web.

## When do you need to convert an image?

Conversion solves problems that format mismatch creates. Common situations:

- A **PNG file is too large** for a website - JPG or WEBP will shrink it dramatically
- A **logo has a white background** from a PNG to JPG conversion - you actually want to keep transparency
- An **iPhone photo (HEIC) will not open** in Windows, email, or an upload form - convert to JPG
- A **WEBP image looks wrong** in an old tool or CMS - convert to PNG or JPG
- A **screenshot needs transparency** or editing - convert JPG to PNG
- A **website is loading slowly** because of heavy images - convert to WEBP

Almost every conversion task falls into two buckets: making a file smaller, or making a file more compatible. Understanding which bucket you are in tells you the right output format.

## The four formats you need to know

| Format | Compression | Transparency | File size | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| JPG | Lossy | No | Small | Photos, sharing, websites |
| PNG | Lossless | Yes | Large | Logos, text, screenshots, graphics |
| WEBP | Both | Yes | Smallest | Modern websites, photos |
| HEIC | Lossy (highly efficient) | Yes | Very small | iPhone and Apple photos |

JPG is the universal format for photographs. PNG is the safe choice for anything with sharp edges, text, or transparency. WEBP combines the best of both and wins on file size. HEIC is Apple's efficient format for phone photos - efficient, but poorly supported outside Apple's ecosystem.

<InfoBox type="warning" title="The transparency rule">
The biggest conversion mistake is moving a transparent PNG to JPG. JPG cannot store transparency, so transparent areas become a solid block - usually white. If the image has a logo, icon, or cutout, keep it PNG or use WEBP, both of which preserve transparency.
</InfoBox>

## PNG to JPG: when the file is too big

PNG stores images losslessly, which makes it heavy - especially for photos. Converting PNG to JPG is the fastest way to make a photo-based file dramatically smaller and universally shareable.

<ToolCTA slug="png-to-jpg" title="Convert PNG to JPG in your browser" label="Open PNG to JPG" />

A quick example of the size difference you can expect:

| Source | Format | Typical size |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phone photo | PNG | 3 - 6 MB |
| Same photo | JPG | 300 - 800 KB |
| Same photo | WEBP | 200 - 600 KB |

That is often a 5-10x reduction just from the format change. If your PNG has no transparency and is mostly photographic, converting to JPG is the right move.

## JPG to PNG: when you need quality or transparency

JPG to PNG is the reverse journey, and it is the right choice when you need transparency, a lossless copy, or an image you plan to edit repeatedly without quality loss building up.

<ToolCTA slug="jpg-to-png" title="Convert JPG to PNG in your browser" label="Open JPG to PNG" />

Use it for:

- **Adding transparency** - not directly, but a PNG result is ready for tools that need it
- **Graphics and screenshots** - text and sharp edges stay crisp
- **Editing workflows** - each save as PNG adds no further quality loss

<InfoBox type="info" title="PNG will be larger">
Converting JPG to PNG does not magically recover detail the JPG already lost, and the PNG file will be larger. Choose PNG when the benefits - transparency, lossless saving, sharp edges - matter more than file size.
</InfoBox>

## HEIC to JPG: iPhone photos everywhere

If you own an iPhone, most of your photos are HEIC (or HEIF). The format is efficient and beautiful on Apple devices, but Windows apps, older websites, some email clients, and many upload forms simply will not open it.

<ToolCTA slug="heic-to-jpg" title="Convert HEIC photos to JPG" label="Open HEIC to JPG" />

Converting HEIC to JPG makes your photos work anywhere while keeping near-original quality - the standard fix before sending photos to someone on another platform or uploading to a service that rejects HEIC.

## Converting WEBP: to PNG, to JPG, and to WEBP

WEBP is the modern web format: smaller than JPG and PNG at equal quality, with support in every major browser. Two conversion directions matter:

- **Image to WEBP** - when you want the smallest possible file for a website
- **WEBP to PNG or JPG** - when a tool, plugin, or old system does not accept WEBP

<ToolCTA slug="image-to-webp" title="Convert any image to WEBP" label="Open Image to WEBP" />

For websites, converting your hero images and photos to WEBP is one of the highest-impact optimizations available - the files are typically 25-35% smaller than JPG with identical visual quality. Use the **WEBP Converter** when you need to go the other way.

<InfoBox type="success" title="WEBP keeps transparency">
Unlike JPG, WEBP supports transparency. A logo or PNG with a transparent background converts cleanly to WEBP, so you get small files and transparency at the same time.
</InfoBox>

## Common image conversion mistakes

- **Converting transparent PNG to JPG** - transparency becomes a white (or black) box
- **Choosing PNG for photos** - huge files for no visible gain
- **Saving and re-saving JPG repeatedly** - each save applies more lossy compression
- **Forgetting HEIC cannot open everywhere** - convert before you share, not after the recipient complains
- **Converting a large image without resizing** - the format changes but dimensions stay huge
- **Using upload-based converters for private images** - prefer local, browser-based tools

## Which format should you choose?

A quick decision guide for the most common situations:

- **Website photo** - WEBP (or JPG for maximum compatibility)
- **Logo or icon** - PNG or WEBP, keep transparency
- **Email attachment** - JPG, compressed to a reasonable size
- **iPhone photo for someone on Windows** - JPG
- **Screenshot for a document** - PNG for crisp text
- **Print** - high-quality JPG or the original file
